What is not equal between the engines in this picture:

- 8 of 14 groups composite in linear light in shape-cpu's picture (the colour-glyph lowering's choice); Blend2D and Skia composite every group in the target's encoded values, so where translucent layers overlap in those groups the pixels differ by construction
- Blend2D has no hue, saturation, color or luminosity operator: 1 hue group drawn source-over there; Skia draws them
- 2 clips to a glyph outline: Skia clips to the path; Blend2D clips to rectangles only, so each is a layer multiplied by the coverage of the path (a different antialiasing of the clip's edge)
- 1 sweep gradient over part of a turn: Skia states them; Blend2D's conic gradient spans whole turns, so its color line is sampled into stops over one turn (the scene's seam, folds and jumps included)
- 1 radial gradient with a start circle of nonzero radius (a two-circle cone): shape-cpu and Skia state it; Blend2D through its start radius
- 1 dashed stroke: this Blend2D draws a dash array solid, through the context and through BLPath::add_stroked_path alike (the dash array in BLStrokeOptions), so its picture has solid strokes where shape-cpu and Skia (SkDashPathEffect) have dashes, and its time is for solid strokes
- Gradients are interpolated in the target's encoded values straight; a gradient shape-cpu interpolates in linear light is sampled into 16 encoded stops in the scene (the nearest the references have)
- Text is the outlines of the font library's own outline provider drawn as paths by Blend2D, Skia and, in the scene arm, shape-cpu: shaping, outline loading and glyph caches are not timed. Skia's and Blend2D's antialiasing and curve flattening are their own
